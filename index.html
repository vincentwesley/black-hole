How it works

Every pixel follows a ray of light

Bent light

For each pixel the shader sends a light ray backwards from the camera and integrates its path through the Schwarzschild geometry. In units where the Schwarzschild radius rs = 1 the orbit obeys u″ + u = 3⁄2 u² with u = 1/r; written as a 3D acceleration x″ = −3⁄2 h² x / r⁵, a leapfrog step works directly in Cartesian space. Rays with impact parameter below 3√3⁄2 rs fall in; those just above circle the photon sphere at 1.5 rs and draw the thin photon ring.

The disk

A thin disk runs from the innermost stable orbit (3 rs) outward. Its temperature follows the Novikov–Thorne flux of Page & Thorne, which vanishes at the inner edge and peaks near 4.8 rs. Each time a ray crosses the disk plane the shader adds the gas's emission, so the far side of the disk appears lifted over the top and under the bottom of the shadow.

Doppler beaming

Gas orbits at the Keplerian rate. A ray's frequency ratio g = √(1 − 3M/r) / (1 − Ωλ) combines gravitational redshift, time dilation and the Doppler shift. Because Iν/ν³ is conserved, a blackbody at temperature T is seen as a blackbody at gT, so the approaching side turns brighter and bluer while the receding side dims and reddens. The Film look switches these shifts off, as the makers of Interstellar did.

Real sky, real colours

The background holds the 9,096 stars of the Yale Bright Star Catalogue, placed by galactic coordinates with the galactic centre behind the hole and coloured from their B−V index, over NASA's Gaia-based map of the Milky Way's diffuse light, scaled to its real surface brightness relative to the stars. Disk and star colours come from Planck spectra integrated against the CIE 1931 colour matching functions, then bloom and an ACES tone curve finish the frame.
